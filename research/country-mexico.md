## 1. MIGRATION PROFILE

### 1.1 Size and trend
- Mexico-born US population: 2.2M (1980); peak above 11.7M (2007); about 11.1M (2024); 22% of all 50.2M US immigrants (2024). Source: MPI 2024 profile as relayed by Mexico News Daily, https://mexiconewsdaily.com/news/mexico-born-population-of-us-drops-to-11-1-million/ (primary: https://www.migrationpolicy.org/article/mexican-immigrants-united-states-2024). LIKELY (secondary relay of MPI).
- Mexico-born number fell about 5% 2010 to 2024 while total immigrants rose 26%. Source: MPI spotlight via search summary, https://www.migrationpolicy.org/journal/spotlight/mexican-immigrants-united-states-2024. LIKELY.
- Mexican share of US immigrants fell from 29% (2010) to 22% (2023). Source: Pew, via search summary (Pew page not fetched). LIKELY.
- Net migration: Pew found more Mexicans left the US than arrived for much of 2005-2014 (https://www.migrationpolicy.org/print/18043 and Pew 2015 report). MPI says outflow exceeded inflow in the years since 2008 (https://www.migrationpolicy.org/commentary/diverse-flows-drive-increase-us-unauthorized-immigrant-population). LIKELY. Net flow since 2015 is not quantified in my sources: UNVERIFIED (I did not find a year-by-year net migration series).
- 2025: total US immigrant population fell from 53.3M (Jan 2025) to 51.9M (June 2025) per Pew analysis of Census data; foreign-born share 15.8% to 15.4%. Source: https://www.theepochtimes.com/us/us-immigrant-population-drops-for-first-time-in-six-decades-pew-report-5904763 (secondary). LIKELY. Claim that most who left were Mexican: UNVERIFIED.
- A headline dated 2026-08-03 says the Mexican immigrant count fell 5% in 2025, first fall since 1980 (https://www.abc.com.py/internacionales/2026/08/03/la-cifra-de-inmigrantes-mexicanos-en-estados-unidos-cae-un-5-en-eeuu-por-primera-vez-desde-1980/); body not read. UNVERIFIED.

## 2. TEN CAREER PATHS

Common legal ladder facts used below (labels apply to all paths):
- Immediate relatives (spouse, unmarried child under 21, parent of a US citizen 21+) have no visa queue and may adjust under INA 245(a) if "inspected and admitted or paroled" and not otherwise barred. Entering without inspection (EWI) normally blocks 245(a). LIKELY, https://www.uscis.gov/green-card/green-card-eligibility/green-card-for-immediate-relatives-of-us-citizen.
- INA 245(i): adjust despite EWI/unauthorized work only if a petition or labor certification was filed on your behalf by April 30, 2001 (and if filed after Jan 14, 1998, you were physically present Dec 21, 2000). Pays a $1,000 penalty. So it helps almost nobody who arrived 2015-2026 unless they derive from an older petition. LIKELY, https://www.uscis.gov/laws-and-policy/legislation/immigration-and-nationality-act (INA 245(i)).
- Unlawful presence bars (INA 212(a)(9)(B)): 180 days to under 1 year, then departure, triggers 3-year bar; 1 year or more triggers 10-year bar. Permanent bar for re-entering without inspection after a year of unlawful presence (212(a)(9)(C)). LIKELY, https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual.
- I-601A provisional waiver: for someone outside 245(a) reach who has a USC or LPR spouse or parent facing extreme hardship if the bar applies; applicant files in the US, then attends the consular interview in Ciudad Juarez. Does not work for the 212(a)(9)(C) permanent bar, and children are not qualifying relatives. LIKELY, https://www.uscis.gov/forms/all-forms/form-i-601a (verify; fee and processing times changed).
- Cancellation of removal for non-LPRs (INA 240A(b)): defence in immigration court; needs 10 years continuous presence, good moral character, no certain convictions, and "exceptional and extremely unusual hardship" to a USC/LPR spouse, parent or child; capped at 4,000 grants per fiscal year. Only reachable if you are already in removal proceedings, so a person who arrived 2015 first becomes eligible around 2025. LIKELY, https://www.justice.gov/eoir.
- Family preference wait times for Mexico (November 2026 Visa Bulletin, final action dates, secondary reports of the official bulletin): F1 22 Sep 2008; F2A 22 Apr 2026 reported (looks anomalously current and may reflect a bulletin error or snippet error; verify); F3 15 Jul 2001; F4 22 Apr 2001; F2B Mexico date not found. Sources: https://www.visalawyerblog.com/november-2026-visa-bulletin-key-changes-for-employment-based-and-family-sponsored-categories/ ; https://manifestlaw.com/news/november-2026-visa-bulletin-released-10-22-2026 ; official: https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/legal/visa-law0/visa-bulletin/2027/visa-bulletin-for-november-2026.html (403). UNVERIFIED (secondary), but the order of magnitude (F1 about 18 years, F3 about 25 years, F4 about 25 years) is LIKELY and good for gameplay.
- TN rules: USMCA Annex 2, Appendix 2 list of professions (about 60+, includes accountants, engineers, nurses (RN), scientists, computer systems analysts, management consultants; typically a bachelor's or license); no cap; no labor certification; initial admission up to 3 years, renewable indefinitely; Mexican citizens apply for a TN visa at a consulate (Canadians do not); dual intent is not recognised so green card plans can cause trouble at the border; spouses/children get TD (no work). Reports of 4 or 5 year visa validity for Mexicans exist (https://visaserve.com/tn-visa-eligibility-for-mexican-citizens-has-expanded-to-four-years-an-overview-by-the-npz-law-group/): UNVERIFIED. Rules: LIKELY, https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/tn-nafta-professionals.
- H-2A (seasonal agriculture; no cap; employer must show no US workers; up to 3 years total, then 3 months abroad; tied to one employer; employer provides housing, transport reimbursement) and H-2B (non-agricultural seasonal/peakload; 66,000 cap plus supplements; up to 3 years). Neither gives a path to a green card by itself (no dual intent). Sources: https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/agriculture/h2a ; https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/h-2b-temporary-non-agricultural-workers. LIKELY.
- ITIN: IRS Form W-7 issues taxpayer IDs to people not eligible for SSNs; allows tax filing, some banking and credit. Does not authorize work or give status. LIKELY, https://www.irs.gov/individuals/individual-taxpayer-identification-number. IRS-ICE data sharing agreement (2025) raised fears of using ITINs: UNVERIFIED here.
- Driver's licences without proof of status: 19 states plus DC (CA, CO, CT, DE, HI, IL, MD, MA, MN, NV, NJ, NM, NY, OR, RI, UT, VT, VA, WA). Source: https://wjla.com/news/nation-world/19-states-and-dc-allow-undocumented-immigrants-to-obtain-drivers-licenses-illegal-immigration-migrants-president-donald-trump (cites NILC). LIKELY. Other states (including Texas, Georgia, Florida, North Carolina, Arizona): no licence for undocumented; driving unlicensed risks arrest and ICE referral. (Utah issues driving privilege cards: LIKELY.)
- Health access: undocumented adults generally cannot enrol in Medicaid, ACA marketplace plans or Medicare; emergency Medicaid, community health centres and county hospital programs exist; some states (CA, IL, OR, MN, NY, WA, DC) extend state-funded coverage to some groups, with several scaling back in 2025-26. LIKELY, https://www.kff.org/racial-equity-and-health-policy/ (not fetched). LPRs wait 5 years for most federal benefits. LIKELY.

Arrival-year availability (applies to every path; DACA = never):
- Arrived 2015-2016: now 10 to 11 years in the US; cancellation of removal clock is mature if placed in proceedings; a marriage to a USC works only if admitted (e.g. on a B-2 visa and not EWI).
- Arrived 2017-2021: 5 to 9 years; can plan family petition from a USC child only when the child turns 21 (child petitions parent; parent must have been admitted, or use I-601A for the consular route).
- Arrived 2022-2026: little protection; asylum rarely granted to Mexicans; expedited removal can apply to people in the US under 2 years (expanded 2025: LIKELY, https://www.federalregister.gov/ search "expedited removal 2025", not verified).

### Path 1. Construction worker, undocumented or family-sponsored (framer, drywall, roofer, laborer)
- Entry/ladder: EWI or B-2 overstay, then (a) USC spouse/child later: if admitted, 245(a) adjustment; if EWI, consular process after I-601A (3/10-year bar waived on hardship), or (b) stay unauthorized and work toward a contractor business, or (c) cancellation of removal after 10 years if placed in proceedings. LPR via F4/F3 from a sibling/married child: decades. LIKELY.
- Employers: residential framers and subcontractors, roofing crews, drywall firms. Clusters: Houston, Dallas, Los Angeles/Riverside, Phoenix, Chicago, Atlanta, Charlotte, Las Vegas, Florida. LIKELY.
- Pay (BLS, May 2023 means unless noted): construction laborers national median $21.78/hr (year unstated on page), Houston mean $19.08/hr, Los Angeles mean $29.14/hr (https://www.bls.gov/oes/current/oes472061.htm ; https://www.bls.gov/oes/2023/may/oes472061.htm) CONFIRMED. Helpers, roofers national median $18.57/hr (https://www.bls.gov/oes/current/oes473016.htm) CONFIRMED. Chicago: 2020 mean $32.05/hr (union scale; stale), UNVERIFIED for current. Unauthorized pay is typically below these because of cash/piece rates and wage theft: UNVERIFIED magnitude.
- Risks: falls and injuries (construction is a leading sector for fatal injuries: BLS CFOI, https://www.bls.gov/iif/), wage theft by subcontractors, worksite ICE operations, employers firing after E-Verify, no workers' comp enforcement. LIKELY.
- Milestones: crew lead, small subcontractor with ITIN-based LLC, buy a home with ITIN mortgage, legalize via USC child at 21, a union card. LIKELY.

### Path 2. Farmworker, H-2A guest worker (Mexico supplies most H-2A workers)
- Entry/ladder: recruited in Mexico by an employer or labor contractor, H-2A visa at consulate (Monterrey/Guadalajara/others), seasonal stay up to about 10 months, up to 3 years then 3 months abroad, return annually. No path to green card from the visa itself. Cannot change employers freely. Source for rules: https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/agriculture/h2a. LIKELY.
- Employers/places: Florida, Georgia, California, Washington, North Carolina (top states FY2025, DOL Q4 table, CONFIRMED). Crops: citrus, vegetables, berries, tobacco, apples.
- Pay: 2026 AEWR set under the Oct 2025 interim final rule; DOL says wages range about $8 to $17/hr under the new tiers versus $15 to $20 in 2025 (https://www.washingtonlawhelp.org/en/h-2a-wages-department-labor-2025-adverse-effect-wage-rate-aewr-change ; https://mwakilishi.com/article/immigration-news/2025-12-03/h-2a-farm-nonimmigrant-workers-could-see-drop-in-minimum-hourly). LIKELY. A court ordered the 2026-27 AEWR process redone (Aug 2026, scope unclear): UNVERIFIED (https://fruitgrowersnews.com/news/dol-updates-h2a-adverse-effect-wage-rates/). Three metro wage rows are not meaningful for ag; use state AEWR instead (CA, WA, FL).
- Health/ID: H-2A workers are in lawful status; they cannot get a SSN card for non-work purposes beyond work authorization; licences follow state rules (a lawful presence document qualifies in most states). Workers' comp and housing inspection apply. LIKELY.
- Risks: employer retaliation and blacklisting, visa fee/recruiter debt, heat illness, housing quality, overstay leading to unlawful presence and the 3/10-year bars. LIKELY.
- Milestones: return-worker status every year, savings and home building in Mexico (remittances), becoming a supervisor/crew leader, family petition from a USC relative. LIKELY.
- Variant: undocumented farmworker (about 40%+ of crop workers historically; NAWS: https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/national-agricultural-workers-survey, exact current share UNVERIFIED) in California's Central Valley, Salinas, Yakima, with different risks: raids, no protection against wage theft.

### Path 3. Restaurant worker to owner (dishwasher, line cook, taqueria owner)
- Entry/ladder: B-2 overstay or EWI; job through relatives; later USC spouse/child; business ownership possible without status (ITIN, LLC), though licences and leases are harder. Cities: Los Angeles, Houston, Chicago, Dallas, New York, Atlanta, Charlotte. LIKELY.
- Pay: UNVERIFIED for 3 metros (BLS "dishwashers" and "cooks, restaurant" OEWS: https://www.bls.gov/oes/current/oes_stru.htm). Use design range: dishwasher at or near local minimum wage, line cook above it; tipped-wage rules vary by state.
- Risks: wage theft and off-the-clock work (a documented problem in restaurants), burns/cuts, E-Verify and audits (I-9) for owners, loss of lease, sales-tax/payroll compliance. LIKELY.
- Milestones: sous-chef, food truck, own restaurant with ITIN, hiring relatives, first lawful hire.

### Path 4. Landscaping business owner
- Entry/ladder: crew member for a company, then own mower-and-truck business; H-2B employers in landscaping are major users of the cap (whether Mexican workers: LIKELY). Own-account business is possible with an ITIN; commercial insurance and licences are the barrier. Clusters: Texas, Florida, Georgia, Carolinas, Southern California, suburban Chicago. LIKELY.
- Pay: UNVERIFIED metro. BLS "landscaping and groundskeeping workers" 37-3011 (https://www.bls.gov/oes/current/oes373011.htm) for the three metros not retrieved.
- Risks: truck and trailer stops (unlicensed driving in 31 states), injuries, seasonality, payment delays, landlord/HOA disputes, worksite enforcement in residential areas.
- Milestones: second crew, commercial contracts, trucks and equipment financing, home ownership, lawful status via a USC child.

### Path 5. Truck driver / logistics worker
- Entry/ladder: CDL requires lawful status in most states; since the 2025 federal rule, only LPRs and some visa categories can hold non-domiciled CDLs; English proficiency rules are enforced at roadside (FMCSA 2025). Sources: https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/ (rules not re-verified in this session). UNVERIFIED details. Undocumented workers are more likely warehouse workers, forklift drivers, last-mile couriers or dock workers. Ladder: warehouse/dock job, later LPR (family), CDL, owner-operator authority (needs capital).
- Clusters: Laredo and El Paso (cross-border freight), Dallas-Fort Worth, Houston, Inland Empire, Chicago. LIKELY.
- Pay: UNVERIFIED metro (BLS heavy and tractor-trailer drivers 53-3032, warehouse 53-7065 https://www.bls.gov/oes/current/oes537065.htm).
- Risks: rule changes can strand a CDL holder; accidents, hours-of-service pressure, misclassification as independent contractors, detention at inspection points.
- Milestones: CDL, owner-operator truck, own small carrier.

### Path 6. Home health aide to nurse (healthcare)
- Entry/ladder: LPR (family) then CNA/LPN, then RN. Hispanic and Mexican-born women are overrepresented among aides; sector pay is low. Medicaid funding cuts (2025 budget law) are a risk to employers: UNVERIFIED specifics. A Mexican RN abroad could instead enter via TN (RN is an Annex-listed profession; needs state licence and CGFNS credential review). LIKELY.
- Clusters: California, Texas, Illinois, Arizona.
- Pay: UNVERIFIED metro (BLS home health aides 31-1121 https://www.bls.gov/oes/current/oes311121.htm). Use design bands: aide near local minimum wage; RN far higher.
- Risks: back injuries, unpaid hours in 24-hour live-in arrangements, license barriers for unauthorized workers (most boards require SSN or lawful presence; some states permit ITIN-based licences: LIKELY but verify per state), foreign credential recognition.
- Milestones: NCLEX pass, RN, nurse manager, home-care agency ownership.

### Path 7. TN professional (accountant, engineer, IT/systems analyst, nurse)
- Entry/ladder: job offer from a US employer, bachelor's degree or licence in a USMCA-listed profession, TN visa at a consulate (about 15,672 TN visas worldwide FY2024, mostly Mexican; CONFIRMED worldwide), admission up to 3 years, renewable. Green card route: employer PERM, EB-2/EB-3 (Mexico is not oversubscribed for EB in the way India/China are, but the worldwide EB queues apply; the Nov 2026 EB dates not retrieved: UNVERIFIED). The TN holder who files an I-140 without a clear temporary intent risks denial at the border. LIKELY.
- Cities: Dallas, Houston, Austin, Atlanta, Chicago, San Jose, Detroit (autos), Phoenix. LIKELY.
- Pay: UNVERIFIED metro. BLS (software developers, accountants, mechanical engineers) vary roughly with US wages; design placeholder: lower-middle to upper-middle of US professional pay.
- Risks: employer dependence, 3-year renewal friction at the border or consulate (a TN approval rate fell to 57% in FY2024 per a Reddit-cited figure: UNVERIFIED), no spouse work authorization (TD), policy shifts such as USMCA review in 2026.
- Milestones: green card via PERM, spouse job, home purchase, naturalization after 5 years of LPR.

### Path 8. International student (F-1) to professional
- Entry/ladder: F-1 visa (needs ability to pay and non-immigrant intent), OPT up to 12 months (24 more for STEM), then H-1B lottery or O-1 or employer sponsorship, or TN if eligible (a common Mexican route: F-1, then TN, then green card). Mexican student numbers: UNVERIFIED (IIE Open Doors: https://opendoorsdata.org). Tuition as an out-of-state/international student is high; scholarships such as Fulbright and university grants exist. Undocumented students may pay in-state tuition in about 20+ states (NCSL; LIKELY, not verified in this session).
- Clusters: Texas, California, Florida, Illinois, Michigan, Massachusetts.
- Risks: visa denial (214(b)), losing status through course violations, H-1B lottery (a new $100,000 fee on new H-1B petitions from abroad was announced Sept 2025: UNVERIFIED, check USCIS), social-media vetting, family debt.
- Milestones: STEM OPT, TN or H-1B, green card, startup.

### Path 9. Caregiver / domestic worker / nanny (live-in)
- Entry/ladder: often informal (EWI or overstay); lawful routes limited. A-3/G-5 or B-1 as a domestic worker accompanying an employer exist but are narrow. Unauthorized domestic workers have very limited route to status unless a USC spouse or child. LIKELY.
- Clusters: Los Angeles, Houston, Dallas, Miami, New York, Chicago, DC suburbs.
- Pay: UNVERIFIED. Domestic workers are excluded from some federal protections historically; several states (CA, IL, NY, OR, NV, etc.) have Domestic Workers' Bills of Rights. LIKELY.
- Risks: isolation, abuse by employers, unpaid overtime, trafficking (T/U visas exist for victims), no health coverage.
- Milestones: written contract, steady households, own cleaning company, U-visa or T-visa where applicable.

### Path 10. Factory / meatpacking / food-processing worker, family-sponsored LPR
- Entry/ladder: LPR through a USC spouse (immediate relative) or sibling/parent petition (long queues: F4 about 25 years).
