# India: Add-ons for "Immigrant" (universal routes, informal work, city notes, challenges)

Compiled 2026-11-08. Companion to `country-india.md` (read first; nothing from it is repeated except where a number is corrected). Social-science reference for game design, not legal advice, and not a how-to. It describes sectors, pay, risks and outcomes from published data. It contains no guidance on evading law enforcement or obtaining false documents.

Labels: CONFIRMED = I read the number in the cited primary file or page this session (I downloaded and parsed the PDFs). LIKELY = secondary source (news, law firm, think tank summary) or a well-known rule I did not re-read at the primary page. UNVERIFIED = could not be checked; do not show to players as fact.

Writer note: Indian immigrants are not one profile. The same file covers physicians, students, motel owners, nurses and truck drivers. Most Indian-born people in the US are lawfully present. The informal-work sections describe a minority and describe employers who exploit as often as workers who take risks. Do not tie any sector, region, language group or religion to wrongdoing. Where a court or agency found a violation, the finding is about that employer, not about a community.

Extraction note: the State Department and DHS PDFs below are blocked from direct download (Cloudflare 403) but were read through web.archive.org copies, which are the same files. I parsed them with a script and cross-checked: FY2024 F-1 (86,067) matches press reports of "about 86,000", FY2023 F-1 (130,730) matches "about 131,000", FY2022 F-1 (115,115) matches "about 115,000".

### 1.3 Route by route

Frequency key for game: COMMON = tens of thousands a year; MODERATE = thousands; RARE = under 100 a year or ineligible.

**B-1/B-2 visitor: COMMON (largest flow by far, up to 898,754 issued FY2024)**
- Profile: family visits (parents visiting adult children in the US is a standard use), business trips, tourism, wedding and funeral travel. Applicants skew toward people with property, jobs or prior US travel; first-time young single applicants are the group most often refused (rule of thumb from the nonimmigrant intent test; India-specific refusal profile data NOT FOUND, UNVERIFIED).
- Wait times: reported roughly 4 months (Kolkata) to 12 months (Hyderabad) in September 2026 (already in the existing file, LIKELY).
- Overstay data, DHS Entry/Exit reports (CONFIRMED, I parsed the tables; air and sea ports only, so land-border and Canada/Mexico excluded):

**F-1 student: COMMON (130,730 FY2023; 86,067 FY2024; sharp 2025 fall)**
- Profile: mostly graduate (MS) students in STEM, then bachelor's; self-funded or loan-funded (loan amounts NOT VERIFIED). Existing file has Open Doors enrollment, OPT counts (143,740 Indian OPT in 2024-25, LIKELY).
- Overstay data, F/M/J combined, India (CONFIRMED, same reports): FY2018 150,954 expected, 5,716 total overstays, 3.79%; FY2019 159,708, 5,304, 3.32% (suspected in-country 1.92%); FY2021 121,762, 1,160, 0.95%; FY2022 134,902, 5,037, 3.73% (3.12% in-country); FY2023 151,719, 7,081, 4.67% (3.83% in-country); FY2024 179,138, 7,730, 4.32% (3.66% in-country). All countries FY2024 F/M/J: 3.23% total, 2.45% in-country.
  So India F/M/J sits above the all-country average in FY2024 (4.32% vs 3.23%). Same upper-bound caveat. Many "overstays" are people who moved to H-1B, OPT or another status that the system did not match, so treat as ceiling.
- F-1 to H-1B: students use OPT (12 months) plus STEM OPT (24 months) and up to three H-1B registrations. Rule: 90 days aggregate unemployment on OPT, 150 with STEM OPT; SEVP terminates the record automatically around 90 days without reported employment; since 2025 ICE sends warning notices. LIKELY. https://www.bu.edu/isso/?p=15880 ; https://www.klaskolaw.com/ice-issues-unemployment-warnings-to-f-1-students-on-opt/
- Study of Indian-born international graduates: about one third leave the US labor market (already in existing file).

**J-1 exchange: MODERATE (about 8,000 to 12,300 a year)**
- Profile: research scholars, physicians in residency (ECFMG-sponsored), interns/trainees, camp counselors, au pairs (few).
- Two-year home residence rule (INA 212(e)) triggers: government funding, the Exchange Visitor Skills List, or graduate medical education. CONFIRMED change: the State Department revised the Skills List effective 9 December 2024, removing India (with China, Malaysia, Saudi Arabia, South Africa, South Korea, UAE and 20+ others; list shrank from 82 to 45 countries), applied retroactively. For Indians the "skills list" trigger is gone, but government funding and medical training still trigger the requirement. https://www.fragomen.com/insights/united-states-dos-issues-revised-j-1-exchange-visitor-skills-list-with-retroactive-effect-china-and-india-among-other-countries-have-been-removed-from-the-list.html
- Physicians use the waiver route (Conrad 30, already in existing file).
- J-1 overstay: included in the F/M/J figures above; J-only rate NOT FOUND.

**M-1 vocational: RARE (489 to 969 a year)**
- Profile: short vocational/technical programs (aviation, culinary, technical diplomas). Not a path to OPT-STEM. Overstay rate for M alone not broken out. UNVERIFIED profile details.

**O-1: MODERATE and rising (324 in FY2015 to 1,566 in FY2024; O-2 622)**
- Profile: researchers, founders, artists, athletes, entertainers; used as an alternative when the H-1B lottery fails. Evidence-heavy; no nationality refusal rate found. A path to EB-1A (existing file). Approval rates for O-1 by nationality NOT FOUND.

**P (P-1, P-3): RARE for P-1 (3 to 73), MODERATE for P-3 (2,065 in FY2015, 32 in FY2021, 2,879 in FY2024)**
- Profile: P-3 is for artists/entertainers in culturally unique programs (touring classical, folk and devotional performers); P-1 athletes and entertainment groups. Usually short-term tours, not a settlement route. The jump in P-3 visas in FY2023 to FY2024 is as plausible as a tour-season rebound as anything else; reason UNVERIFIED.

**R-1 religious worker: MODERATE (769 to 1,320 a year)**
- Profile: priests, temple staff, gurdwara and mosque staff, religious teachers. Maximum 5 years; then a special immigrant (EB-4) path with its own backlog (EB-4 India dates not retrieved). LIKELY.
- Documented abuse: see 2.2, job 4 (the Robbinsville case concerned workers on religious-worker and other visas; the allegations are in a lawsuit and the DOJ later closed its probe).

**L-1 intracompany transferee: COMMON (18,000 to 25,500 a year; L-2 dependents 21,000 to 32,000)**
- Profile: Indian IT firms' staff plus Indian affiliates of US firms. L-1A (managers/executives) can reach EB-1C; L-1B (specialized knowledge) is capped at 5 years. LIKELY.
- Spouses on L-2 may work without a separate permit (a long-standing rule), unlike H-4 without EAD. LIKELY. https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/l-1a-intracompany-transferee-executive-or-manager

**E-1/E-2 treaty: RARE and mostly not available to Indian passports**
- India is not an E-1/E-2 treaty country. CONFIRMED by multiple law-firm lists (travel.state.gov treaty page blocked; check there before shipping). https://www.lighthousehq.com/blog/e2-treaty-countries ; https://www.beyondborderglobal.com/resources/e-2-visa-countries
- The table above still shows E-2 visas issued to Indian nationals (11 to 79 a year, E-1 0 to 2). I cannot explain why; plausible explanations are dependents or employees who hold a treaty-country status, or recording by nationality of place of birth. UNVERIFIED. For the game, E is effectively unavailable unless the character holds a second nationality (for example a Canadian passport): LIKELY, rule knowledge from the same law-firm pages.

**H-2B seasonal non-agricultural: RARE (0 to 2 a year)**
- India is not on the DHS eligible-country list for H-2A or H-2B. LIKELY (Business Standard 2025 and 2026 reports); a case-by-case national-interest exception exists. https://www.business-standard.com/immigration/us-h-2b-visa-2026-first-allocation-cap-reached-indians-ineligible-126021800662_1.html ; https://eiglaw.com/dhs-and-dos-announce-countries-eligible-for-h-2a-and-h-2b-visa-programs/
- Historic example: Indian welders and pipe-fitters were brought on H-2B by Signal International after Hurricane Katrina (2006 to 2008), before India fell off the eligible list. Legal outcomes in 2.2, job 4. LIKELY. https://www.eeoc.gov/newsroom/signal-international-llc-pay-5-million-settle-eeoc-race-national-origin-lawsuit
- Game rule: H-2B is not available to an Indian character (CONFIRMED by the 0 to 2 per year).

**K-1 fiance(e) visa: MODERATE (413 to 1,128 a year; 1,128 in FY2024, the highest in the series)**
- Profile: US citizen petitioner marrying within 90 days of entry (rule), then adjusting status; a gender and arranged-marriage split is NOT FOUND. UNVERIFIED.
- Rule: marry within 90 days or the K-1 holder must leave; a marriage to someone else does not allow adjustment. LIKELY. https://www.uscis.gov/family/family-of-us-citizens/visas-for-fiances-of-us-citizens

## 2. INFORMAL ("UNDER THE TABLE") WORK FOR OUT-OF-STATUS OR STATUS-LIMITED INDIANS

Scope: people who overstayed, lost status, entered without inspection, or hold a status that bars the job they are doing (for example F-1 working off campus, H-4 without EAD).

## 4. TWENTY MORE CHALLENGE IDEAS (specific to these routes)

Each ties to a number or rule above. Pass/fail describes outcomes, not instructions.

1. **Visitor interview.** Basis: projected B-1/B-2 refusal rates 16.8% to 32.4% at India posts (VisaGrader, LIKELY). Pass: ties (job, property, family) shown and visa issued; Fail: refused under 214(b), reapply next year.
2. **Appointment wait for a parent.** Basis: visitor appointment waits up to 12 months (existing file). Pass: parent gets a slot before the birth or wedding; Fail: event missed, relationship score drops.
3. **Student visa gamble, 2024.** Basis: F-1 refusal projected 41% (FY2024) and issuance fell 38% in Jan to Sep 2024 (LIKELY). Pass: issued; Fail: refused and the admission lapses.
4. **Summer 2025 pause.** Basis: May to August 2025 Indian F-1 issuance 22,149 vs 58,694 (LIKELY) and a late-May interview pause. Pass: interview already booked and issued; Fail: deferred a semester or lost the I-20.
5. **OPT clock.** Basis: 90 days aggregate unemployment, 150 with STEM OPT (LIKELY). Pass: job logged in SEVIS in time; Fail: SEVIS record terminated within about 15 days of the limit.
6. **Unreported OPT job.** Basis: ICE notices sent from 2025 about unreported employment (LIKELY). Pass: updates SEVIS within 15 days; Fail: record ends, status lost.
7. **Pay-to-stay trap.** Basis: Farmington sting: 600+ enrolled, about 250 arrested, about 80% voluntary departure (CONFIRMED event). Pass: checks the school is real and attends classes; Fail: arrest, voluntary departure, a record that follows later visa applications.
8. **J-1 two-year rule.** Basis: India removed from the Skills List 9 December 2024, but government funding and medical training still trigger it (CONFIRMED). Pass: waiver or not bound; Fail: must return home two years before H-1B/green card.
9. **K-1 90-day deadline.** Basis: K-1 issuances rose to 1,128 in FY2024; the marriage must occur within 90 days (LIKELY). Pass: marriage on time, adjustment filed; Fail: relationship falters, status ends.
10. **O-1 evidence build.** Basis: O-1 grew from 324 (FY2015) to 1,566 (FY2024) (CONFIRMED). Pass: expert letters and press support the petition; Fail: denial or RFE, fall back to a salaried visa.
11. **Cultural performer tour.** Basis: P-3 visas 2,879 in FY2024 versus 32 in FY2021 (CONFIRMED). Pass: tour completes and returns on time; Fail: promoter cancels and the group's status ends early.
12. **Religious-worker contract.** Basis: R-1 issuance 769 to 1,320 a year; Robbinsville allegations of about $1.20 an hour (LIKELY). Pass: written pay terms met and wage verified; Fail: wage far below the contract and a decision on whether to report.
13. **Employer asks for your passport.** Basis: Chesterfield "document servitude" conviction (CONFIRMED). Pass: contact a legal aid or the hotline and recover documents; Fail: dependence deepens.
14. **Recruiter fee debt.** Basis: Signal International workers paid fees and $1,050 a month for camp housing; $14 million jury verdict (CONFIRMED). Pass: recognizes the red flags before paying; Fail: debt that controls choices.
15. **Back-wage claim.** Basis: WHD recovered $200,137 in Oregon (2026) and $55,435 in North Carolina (2023) (CONFIRMED). Pass: files with DOL and recovers pay; Fail: retaliation fears stop the claim and wages stay unpaid (legal protections against retaliation exist; their practical strength is UNVERIFIED).
16. **Delivery-app floor.** Basis: NYC pay floor $22.13 an hour (LIKELY). Pass: the character has valid work authorization and earns the floor; Fail: app deactivation or lower unregulated rates elsewhere.
17. **Roadside English check.** Basis: reported FMCSA out-of-service totals in the thousands (7,000 to 11,000; LIKELY). Pass: licensed driver passes the proficiency assessment; Fail: placed out of service and loses income until retraining.
18. **Overstay clock.** Basis: 180 days = 3-year bar, one year = 10-year bar (LIKELY). Pass: leaves or fixes status before 180 days; Fail: crosses the threshold and a later green card needs a waiver.
19. **Asylum timeline.** Basis: 34,230 Indian cases pending in immigration courts in late 2022 (LIKELY). Pass: filing within the deadline and a grant (about 5,340 reported grants in FY2023); Fail: denial and a removal order.
20. **Tax filing without work permission.** Basis: ITEP estimate of $96.7 billion paid by unauthorized immigrants (CONFIRMED); an ITIN does not authorize work (LIKELY). Pass: files taxes on time and keeps records for later adjustment; Fail: avoids filing and loses the paper trail.

## 5. KNOWN GAPS AND UNCERTAINTIES

1. State Department FY2025 and FY2026 nonimmigrant tables could not be retrieved; FY2019 is "preliminary"; R-1 for FY2019 to FY2021 not extracted. The tables show issued visas by nationality, not admitted people and not USCIS petitions.
2. Refusal rates are not published by nationality. All rates here are VisaGrader projections or a worldwide F-1 figure. Treat as LIKELY at best.
3. FY2015 to FY2017 India rows in the DHS overstay reports were not extracted (only FY2018, FY2019, FY2021 to FY2024), and the FY2020 row was not found. "Suspected in-country overstay" is an upper bound; later records shrink it.
4. No data on how many unauthorized Indians overstayed versus entered without inspection; the JHU SAIS PDF returned HTTP 403. Estimates of the population range from 220,000 (DHS 2022) to 725,000 (Pew 2021).
5. No Indian-specific data on informal pay, informal sectors, or numbers of Indian workers in each. All sector entries rest on enforcement cases (restaurants, hotels, stores, construction, domestic work, salons), which show that violations occur but not how common they are. Absence of cases elsewhere (for example Houston, Chicago) is not evidence of absence.
6. DOL/DOJ pages were mostly seen through search summaries, not opened one by one. Dates for the Cartersville and Robbinsville outcomes were not confirmed. BAPS: allegations only.
7. I-9/E-Verify penalty amounts and ITIN counts not found. The E-Verify state list varies by source.
8. USCIS petition approval rates by nationality (H-1B RFE/denial for India, L-1, O-1, R-1) not retrieved. H-1B India shares are from press: 74.1% (FY2021), 72.6% (FY2022), 72% (FY2023), about 71% (FY2024, existing file).
9. Immigrant-visa tables for FY2018 and FY2020 to FY2024 not retrieved; consular-only counts understate employment-based green cards that are adjusted in the US.
10. November 2026 Visa Bulletin family dates are from law-firm trackers; one entry (F2A "22 Oct 2026") looks odd. Verify at travel.state.gov.
11. Trucking and CDL material is heavily politicized and conflicting; no official count of Indian-born drivers; use only as lawful-work enforcement context.
12. Deportation and border numbers conflict across outlets (1,703 vs 3,258 for 2025; 63,927 vs other FY2022 totals; asylum 41,030 vs 41,330 vs 21,000). Do not show as fact without the official CBP/ICE/EOIR tables.
13. Unauthorized-population sector, state and wage profile for Indians (MPI Data Hub, ACS) not retrieved.
14. Policy shocks (H-1B fee, weighted lottery, student-visa pause, remittance tax, E-Verify changes) move fast; their status on 8 November 2026 is not verified.
15. E-2 visas to Indian nationals (11 to 79 a year) conflict with "India is not a treaty country"; cause unknown.
