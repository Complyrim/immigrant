# Nigeria Add-Ons: Universal Routes, Informal Work, Cities, Challenges

Compiled 2026-11-08. Game-design and social-science reference only, not legal advice. Adds to `country-nigeria.md` (read first; not repeated here). The Diversity Visa (DV) path is treated as dropped.

**Label key:** CONFIRMED = read directly in a primary source file or page I opened this session, or stated identically by several independent sources. LIKELY = consistent secondary reporting, third-party site that republishes official data, or a standard legal rule I did not re-open at the official page. UNVERIFIED = single weak source, background knowledge, or conflicting sources.

**How I got the numbers:** I downloaded and parsed the DHS/CBP Entry/Exit Overstay Reports (FY2018 to FY2024) and the OHSS LPR-by-country-by-class workbook (FY2005 to FY2024) myself, so those tables are CONFIRMED. travel.state.gov, bls.gov (some pages), migrationpolicy.org and the NFAP PDF returned 403 or unreadable output. Visa-issuance counts by class come from visagrader.com, a third-party site that republishes State Department data (LIKELY, not CONFIRMED; its FY2026 column is almost certainly a partial year). Nothing here is invented; where I found no number, I say so.

**Neutrality note:** Nigeria has 250+ ethnic groups and roughly equal Muslim and Christian populations. Migrants are students, nurses, engineers, traders, spouses, clergy, artists and workers. Overstay and enforcement statistics describe a visa-category flow, not the character of any person or community. Where a trafficking case is cited, both victims and offenders were individuals; it says nothing about a group.

### 1.2 Route by route (Nigeria-specific)

Frequency key for game balance: COMMON, UNCOMMON, RARE.

**B-1/B-2 visitor: COMMON as an entry door, but shrinking.**
- Volume: by far the largest class (see table); about 83% of Nigerian NIVs in 2024 (UNVERIFIED).
- Validity and entry limits: from 2025-07-08 most Nigerian nonimmigrant visas are single-entry, 3-month validity under visa reciprocity; earlier-issued visas keep original terms; diplomatic visas unaffected. CONFIRMED by many outlets, e.g. https://allafrica.com/stories/202507090027.html , https://www.channelstv.com/2025/07/08/us-restricts-nigerian-non-immigrant-visas-to-three-months-single-entry , https://guardian.ng/news/u-s-halts-multiple-entry-visa-for-nigerians-reduces-validity-to-three-months/ . The official reciprocity schedule is at https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/reciprocity-by-country/NI.html (not opened; 403 likely). Before July 2025 the long-validity multiple-entry B visa was the norm for Nigerians who were approved (UNVERIFIED; I did not find the pre-2025 schedule text).
- Visa bond: Nigeria added to the B-1/B-2 bond pilot effective 2026-01-21. Pilot amounts $5,000, $10,000 or $15,000 set by the consular officer; applies only to B visas; bonded travelers must use designated airports (Boston, JFK, Washington Dulles; Newark, Atlanta, Chicago O'Hare and Los Angeles added from 2026-01-01); pilot ran 2025-08-20 to 2026-08-05; bond refunded on timely departure or non-use. CONFIRMED: https://www.bu.edu/isso/2026/01/05/b-1-visa-bond-pilot-program-expanded/ ; https://eiglaw.com/state-department-launches-visa-bond-pilot-program-for-high-risk-overstay-countries/ . Permanent rule published 2026-08-03 with tiers $10,000 / $15,000 / $20,000, about 50 countries: CONFIRMED rule (several law-firm and university sources, e.g. https://iso.mit.edu/news/u-s-tourist-visa-bond-program-made-permanent-effective-august-3-2026/ , https://eiglaw.com/dos-finalizes-permanent-visa-bond-program-for-certain-b-1-b-2-travelers/ ); I did not see the final country list, so Nigeria's continued inclusion is LIKELY (Nigerian outlets report it, e.g. https://guardian.ng/?p=2890623). Skift reports visas issued to bond countries fell 83% (https://skift.com/2026/07/31/us-visa-bond-program-final-rule/, LIKELY).
- Typical applicant: relatives visiting family, business buyers, professionals, conference and wedding travelers; also people who intend to study, work or marry later (a legal grey area, see 1.3).
- Overstay data: see section 2.1 (B overstay 5.56% in FY2024 to 21.33% in FY2022).

**F-1 student: COMMON, the main skilled pipeline.**
- Open Doors (students in US higher education, academic year): 10,674 (2015/16), 11,710 (2016/17), about 12,860 (2020/21, sum of category table), about 17,640 (2022/23, sum of category table), 20,029 (2023/24), 21,847 (2024/25, rank 8th). LIKELY, from Open Doors fact sheets and press: https://opendoorsdata.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/OpenDoors_FactSheet_Nigeria_2024.pdf , https://opendoorsdata.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/OD_Nigeria_Fact-Sheet-2025.pdf , https://www.thecable.ng/?p=137385 . The "14,438 in 2021/22" figure in one source equals the FY2022 naturalization count in the earlier file, so it is likely a mix-up (UNVERIFIED). 2017/18 to 2019/20 not found (GAP).
- Issuances to Nigerian nationals peaked at 7,556 (FY22) and fell to 3,939 (FY25), a 48% drop (LIKELY).
- Typical applicant: master's and PhD applicants, often STEM, health, business; many on partial funding; family-financed tuition is common (UNVERIFIED share).
- Rules: OPT 12 months; STEM OPT +24 months (employer must be in E-Verify, have an EIN and a training plan); unemployment cap 90 days on initial OPT, 150 total with STEM extension. CONFIRMED: https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/students-and-exchange-visitors/optional-practical-training-extension-for-stem-students-stem-opt
- F-1 applicants outside the US without a valid visa on 2026-01-01 are barred under Proclamation 10998; students with valid F/J visas are not affected (US Mission statement as reported: https://businessday.ng/news/article/u-s-assures-nigerian-students-with-valid-f1-j1-visas-safe-despite-new-travel-restrictions/ ). CONFIRMED (scope), UNVERIFIED (any later change).
- Overstay data: F/M/J rate 11.90% FY2024 (section 2.1).

**J-1 exchange: UNCOMMON (about 800 to 1,100 issuances a year), but high-value (physicians, researchers, scholars).**
- Nigerians were reported as the 6th-largest group of J-1 foreign doctors in one 2024 database (earlier file, LIKELY).
- Rule: INA 212(e) two-year home-country physical presence requirement applies to many J-1s (government-funded, skills-list, or graduate medical education); waivers include no-objection, hardship, persecution, interested-government-agency, and for physicians the Conrad 30 program (30 waivers per state per year, 3 years of service in an underserved area). LIKELY standard rule; see https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/study/exchange.html and https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/students-and-exchange-visitors/conrad-30-waiver-program (not opened).
- Overstay data: included in F/M/J.

**M-1 vocational student: RARE (32 to 96 Nigerian issuances a year).** Cannot normally change to F-1 once enrolled (LIKELY rule) and has limited work rights; mostly flight school, trades.

**O-1 extraordinary ability: RARE but fastest-growing among work visas.** Issuances to Nigerian nationals rose from 35 (FY18) to 154 (FY24) and 149 (FY25) (LIKELY).
- O-1A: sciences, education, business, athletics. O-1B: arts, and motion pictures or television. Standard: sustained national or international acclaim, normally evidence in at least three categories; a peer-group advisory opinion is required; up to 3 years initial, extensions of 1 year. CONFIRMED: https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/o-1-visa-individuals-with-extraordinary-ability-or-achievement
- Arts, entertainment and sports examples (design-level, not data on Nigerians): Afrobeats and Afro-fusion recording artists and producers touring with documented chart, press and award history; Nollywood and film actors, directors, cinematographers with festival awards and distribution; fashion designers; visual artists with museum or gallery exhibitions; novelists and journalists with major-outlet recognition; professional athletes (basketball, soccer, track) with national-team or major-league records (athletes can alternatively use P-1 for team events); tech founders and researchers (O-1A) with patents, press, judging, high-salary evidence. A page with an interview-ready portfolio drives the odds in game terms. No Nigeria-specific approval rate found (GAP).
- O-1 is a bridge to EB-1A or EB-2 NIW (see 1.4 on EB classes).

**P-1/P-2/P-3 athletes and performers: RARE (4 to 50 Nigerian issuances a year).** Short stays tied to a team, group or cultural program; sports exemptions also appear in Proclamation 10998 (athletes for major events; LIKELY, https://thenigerialawyer.com/us-to-impose-partial-visa-restrictions-on-nigerians-from-january-2026-who-is-affected-who-is-exempt-and-what-it-means-for-travelers ).

**R-1 religious worker: UNCOMMON, steady (130 to 195 Nigerian issuances a year).** Clergy and religious staff of US congregations; stay up to 5 years total (LIKELY rule); sponsoring organization must be a bona fide non-profit religious organization. Applicants span Christian denominations and Muslim institutions; no data by faith (GAP).

**L-1 intracompany transferee: UNCOMMON (about 150 to 220 Nigerian nationals a year).** Nigerian banks, telecom, energy and tech companies with US affiliates; managers and specialized-knowledge staff. LIKELY.

**E-1/E-2 treaty trader/investor: NOT AVAILABLE to Nigerians.** Nigeria is not a treaty country. The State Department treaty table (https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/fees/treaty.html) returned 403; a news report listing the 10 African E-2 countries (Cameroon, Congo-Brazzaville, Congo-Kinshasa, Egypt, Ethiopia, Liberia, Morocco, Senegal, Togo, Tunisia) states Nigeria is absent (https://www.legit.ng/people/1730121-us-releases-list-10-african-countries-citizens-qualify-e-2-investor-visa/). The visagrader E-1 page shows a few E-1 visas issued at the Lagos and Abuja posts (2 to 33 a year) but no table for Nigerian nationals, consistent with those going to non-Nigerian treaty nationals. Verdict: LIKELY (resolves the earlier file's UNVERIFIED Path 10 note). Design implication: no E-2 route; use L-1, O-1, EB-5, or green card-based business.

**H-2B temporary non-agricultural worker: RARE.** One H-2B visa to a Nigerian national in FY2024 (LIKELY); the cap is 66,000 a year (33,000 per half) and the FY2027 first-half cap was reached by 2026-09-04. CONFIRMED cap: https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/h-2b-temporary-non-agricultural-workers . Workers may not be charged recruitment fees (same source). Fee demands from "H-2B recruiters" in Nigeria are a scam signal.

For the game: no DV win for a Nigerian-born player in any arrival year 2015 to 2026 except as an accompanying spouse of an eligible-country winner (UNVERIFIED). This changes Path 5 in the earlier file.

### 1.3 How people move between routes (the "ladders")

All rules below are standard law; official pages given for verification; I opened only those marked CONFIRMED.

1. **B to F change of status.** Possible by filing before the B stay expires (Form I-539 or consular process); an officer may question "preconceived intent" if a visitor really planned to study at entry (LIKELY rule, 8 CFR 248 and INA 214(b); https://www.uscis.gov/forms/all-forms/how-do-i-change-my-nonimmigrant-status). Study cannot begin until the change is approved. Game stat: F-1 refusal rate near 41% in FY2024 (UNVERIFIED) makes "enter on B, switch to F" a risky shortcut. B-1/B-2 single-entry visas from July 2025 make it more constrained, because a rejected change means leaving.
2. **F to OPT to STEM OPT to H-1B.** CONFIRMED OPT/STEM rules above. H-1B registration is now wage-weighted (final rule effective 2026-02-27) and a $100,000 fee applies to beneficiaries outside the US without a valid H-1B (earlier file; LIKELY). Nigerian H-1B issuances: 880 to 884 a year (FY24, FY25), up from 391 (FY18). The visa counts only those who consular-process; many Nigerians switch status inside the US without an H-1B visa stamp, so true H-1B approvals are higher (UNVERIFIED; USCIS H-1B Characteristics reports by country of birth, https://www.uscis.gov/tools/reports-and-studies/h-1b-employer-data-hub , not retrieved).
3. **F to green card via marriage or family** (very common in OHSS spouse data).
4. **J-1 to Conrad 30 to H-1B to EB-2/NIW.** Physician track (earlier file Path 2). The 212(e) rule can strand a J-1 who does not secure a waiver.
5. **H-1B to EB-2 or EB-3 green card** (employment-based table above).
6. **O-1 to EB-1A or EB-2 NIW.** Concurrent filing is common (LIKELY).
7. **K-1 to marriage-based adjustment.** 90-day window.
8. **R-1 to EB-4 special immigrant religious worker.** EB-4 for Nigeria: 150 to 360 principals a year, which includes religious workers and other special immigrants (UNVERIFIED breakdown).
9. **Overstayers to adjustment.** An immediate relative of a US citizen who was inspected and admitted can usually adjust despite an overstay (INA 245(c) exception, LIKELY rule, https://www.uscis.gov/green-card/green-card-eligibility/green-card-for-immediate-relatives-of-us-citizen ). Preference-category applicants generally cannot. See section 2.7.

## 2. INFORMAL ("UNDER THE TABLE") WORK FOR OUT-OF-STATUS OR STATUS-LIMITED NIGERIAN IMMIGRANTS

This section is descriptive: what the data say about sectors, pay, and risks. It contains no instructions for evading enforcement or obtaining false documents. Working without authorization is unlawful for the worker and exposes employers; the pay and protection findings below explain why this is dangerous and why most characters should be steered toward the exits in 2.7.

### 2.2 Pay, protections and the legal floor

- Federal minimum wage $7.25; states differ: DC $18.40, New York $17.00 in NYC and suburbs ($16.00 elsewhere), California $16.90, Illinois $15.00, Texas $7.25 (federal), Georgia $5.15 state rate but federally covered employers owe $7.25. CONFIRMED (DOL page as of 2026-11-05): https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/minimum-wage/state
- Wage theft evidence: EPI estimated minimum-wage violations alone cost workers more than $15 billion a year (2.4 million workers losing about $8 billion in the 10 biggest states; Cooper and Kroeger): https://www.epi.org/blog/wage-theft-by-employers-is-costing-u-s-workers-billions-of-dollars-a-year/ (CONFIRMED search result, LIKELY detail). A DOL study of New York and California found non-citizens 1.6 to 3.1 times more likely to suffer a minimum wage violation; the 2009 "Broken Laws, Unprotected Workers" survey found 37% of unauthorized low-wage workers had a minimum wage violation in the prior week vs 24% for authorized immigrants (same EPI page and survey; LIKELY).
- Taxes: ITEP estimates unauthorized immigrants paid $96.7 billion in taxes in 2022, including about $33.9 billion in payroll taxes (Social Security $25.7B, Medicare $6.4B, unemployment insurance $1.8B), https://itep.org/study-undocumented-immigrants-contribute-nearly-100-billion-in-taxes-a-year/ (LIKELY; ITEP is an advocacy think tank). Workers without an SSN can use an ITIN to file; an ITIN does not confer work authorization (LIKELY rule, https://www.irs.gov/individuals/individual-taxpayer-identification-number-itin ). Whether unauthorized Nigerians file at the same rate is NOT FOUND.
- Banking: access varies by bank policy, ID type, and account purpose; I found no authoritative Nigeria-specific data (GAP). A game rule of "limited credit, no mortgage, cash-heavy life" is a design simplification (UNVERIFIED).
- Employer exposure: knowingly hiring an unauthorized worker carries civil penalties of about $716 to $5,724 per worker for a first offense, up to $28,619 for repeat; Form I-9 paperwork errors $288 to $2,861 per form (2025 inflation-adjusted figures from HR sources; LIKELY, https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/general-immigration/1646972/dhs-final-rule-quicker-and-costlier-fines-for-immigration-related-violations ). A June 2025 interim final rule lets ICE assess and collect these faster (same source). E-Verify is mandatory for most private employers in a handful of states (Alabama, Arizona, Mississippi, South Carolina in one 2026 source; up to 9 to 11 states in others, including Georgia in a 2024 list) and for federal contractors and STEM OPT employers; state lists conflict (UNVERIFIED): https://www.paylocity.com/resources/learn/articles/e-verify-states/ . This is why informal work clusters in cash or small-employer settings and rarely in large chains.
- Enforcement context: DOL's Wage and Hour Division enforces FLSA wage rights for covered employees regardless of immigration status (LIKELY standard position, https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/immigration ), but a wage claim can still carry personal risk, which depresses reporting (Broken Laws survey, LIKELY).

### 2.3 Documented informal or semi-informal job options (10 options)

For each: what the data show, pay compared with the legal floor and market median, and main risks. "Nigerian share" is stated when found; otherwise it is flagged as a data gap. National occupation mix for Nigerian immigrants aged 16 to 65 (outside DC, ACS 2016 to 2020 via IPUMS, IIR fact sheet; CONFIRMED): healthcare practitioners and technical 20%, service occupations 20%, management/business/finance 12%, education/legal/community/arts/media 12%, transportation and material moving 8% (https://d101vc9winf8ln.cloudfront.net/documents/47014/original/Nigerian_Immigrants_in_the_DC_Metro_Area.pdf?1689340040 ). This is for all Nigerian immigrants, most of whom are authorized; it is not an informal-work measure.

1. **Home health and personal care aide (private-duty, cash, live-in).** BLS median $34,900 a year, $16.78 an hour (May 2024); a third-party site lists $17.21 for May 2025 (UNVERIFIED): https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/home-health-aides-and-personal-care-aides.htm . Healthcare is Nigerians' top occupation group; one blog cites over 17% of Nigerian immigrant men and 34% of women working in healthcare (UNVERIFIED, unnamed original: https://www.workingimmigrants.com/2023/06/black-african-immigrants-and-healthcare-jobs/ ). Risks: unpaid overtime and 24-hour live-in shifts; DOL proposed on 2025-07-02 to restore the companionship and live-in exemptions for agency employees and told investigators on 2025-07-25 (FAB 2025-4) to stop enforcing the 2013 rule; New York and New Jersey keep stricter state overtime rules; private lawsuits remain possible (LIKELY: https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/employee-rights-labour-relations/1667448/dol-proposes-rule-to-return-home-healthcare-agency-workers-to-flsa-exempt-status ). Also injury without workers' comp, lifting strain, no reference for later licensure. Share informal: NOT FOUND.
2. **Unlicensed private care sitter or "companion" for older adults (non-medical).** Same wage environment as 1 but with no agency; pay often below $16.78 when cash. Risks: client abuse of trust, no schedule protection, accusation risk when a client's family disputes belongings. No Nigerian-specific data (GAP).
3. **Security guard (small contractors).** BLS median $38,020 a year for security guards (May 2025), https://www.bls.gov/ooh/protective-service/security-guards.htm (LIKELY via search). Most states require a guard license and fingerprint background check, which makes unlicensed paid guard work hard; where it occurs it is typically unreported contract work. Risks: no benefits, hazardous night shifts, criminal-record exposure. Nigerian share: NOT FOUND (a "security guard" appears in the earlier file only as an illustrative DV job).
4. **Rideshare or delivery work via borrowed or "rented" driver accounts.** Platforms require an SSN, identity check and vehicle checks; DOJ has charged more than a dozen Brazilian nationals in a nationwide conspiracy to create fraudulent driver accounts with stolen identities and rent them to others (https://www.justice.gov/usao-ma/victim-and-witness-assistance-program/us-v-wemerson-dutra-aguiar-and-us-v-priscila-barbosa-et-al ); NBC Bay Area has reported an underground rental market (https://www.nbcbayarea.com/news/local/new-scam-puts-unchecked-rideshare-drivers-behind-the-wheel/207917/ ); a 2025 business piece covers platforms' crackdown on account sharing (https://money.com/delivery-apps-unauthorized-account-sharing ). Allegations at DFW and O'Hare airports are reported but unproven (https://dallasexpress.com/state/dfw-airport-police-confirm-active-investigation-into-alleged-rideshare-fraud/ ; https://www.fox32chicago.com/news/immigration-arrests-ohare-sat ). Pay: BLS taxi/shuttle/chauffeur median $37,960 ($18.25 an hour) in 2025 and 86% of taxi drivers self-employed (https://www.bls.gov/ooh/transportation-and-material-moving/taxi-drivers-and-chauffeurs.htm ; CONFIRMED). Risks: identity-fraud and conspiracy charges for both the account lender and borrower, uninsured crashes, deactivation without recourse, fees to whoever owns the account, no earnings record. No nationality breakdown found, and the DHS O'Hare list did not name Nigeria (GAP). A legal driver (transportation and material moving is 8% of Nigerian immigrants) is a different story.
5. **Hair braiding and beauty services (shops, home-based, mobile).** BLS cosmetologist/stylist median $17.25 an hour ($35,880), May 2025; tips included; 80% of barbers and 48% of hairstylists are self-employed; all states require licensing (https://www.bls.gov/ooh/personal-care-and-service/barbers-hairstylists-and-cosmetologists.htm ; CONFIRMED). Institute for Justice reports licensing can require up to 2,100 hours for braiders in some states (https://ij.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/Barriers_To_Braiding-1.pdf ; advocacy source); African Communities Together surveyed nearly 350 braiders in New York in 2020 and describes braiding as the most accessible work for many African immigrant women (https://africans.us/node/436 ; advocacy source). Nigerian share: NOT FOUND (West African braiders include Senegalese, Guinean, Ghanaian, Nigerian and others). Risks: fines for unlicensed practice, shop-owner control over booth fees, health-code violations, inability to report mistreatment.
6. **Cleaning and janitorial through contractors.** BLS median $17.71 an hour, $36,840 a year, May 2025 (https://www.bls.gov/ooh/building-and-grounds-cleaning/janitors-and-building-cleaners.htm ; CONFIRMED). Sector of high wage-and-hour violations per the DOL and EPI work in 2.2 (LIKELY). Risks: subcontracting chains that push pay below minimum, unpaid travel time, chemical exposure, sudden termination. Nigerian share: NOT FOUND.
7. **Warehouse, stocking and hand-labor via staffing agencies.** BLS median $18.38 an hour, $38,220 a year (May 2025; https://www.bls.gov/ooh/transportation-and-material-moving/hand-laborers-and-material-movers.htm ; CONFIRMED). Large warehouses and staffing firms use E-Verify more often, so informal access is mostly via small shops and day labor (UNVERIFIED). Risks: shift cancellation, no overtime, injury, agency fees. Nigerian share: NOT FOUND.
8. **Nursing assistant or orderly roles (and unlicensed aide work in nursing homes or assisted living).** BLS: nursing assistants $42,260 a year, orderlies $38,290, combined $41,870 or $20.13 an hour (May 2025; https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/nursing-assistants.htm ; CONFIRMED). Certification is required for nursing-home CNA work; unlicensed assisted-living aide roles exist in some states (UNVERIFIED). Risks: understaffing, injury, wage-and-hour violations in staffing models. Healthcare is the top Nigerian occupation group, so this is a high-probability path into formal work once status is fixed.
9. **Nanny, housekeeper or domestic worker in a private household.** Documented severe-abuse cases involve Nigerian victims brought on false pretenses: Nnaji (Arlington, Texas; couple convicted of forced labor, Civil Rights Division press release https://justice.gov/opa/pr/arlington-texas-couple-convicted-forced-labor-and-other-crimes-holding-nigerian-woman ), Bolarinwa (New Jersey; coerced domestic labor Dec 2015 to Oct 2016, 45-month sentence for one defendant, https://guardian.ng/?p=2595610 ), Katy, Texas (https://www.fox26houston.com/news/katy-couple-accused-of-enslaving-nanny ), Udeozor (Maryland, 2006), and a New York federal civil suit. These are specific cases, not a rate. Risks: confiscated passports, isolation, no pay, threats about immigration status. Victims may qualify for T-visa protection (LIKELY rule, https://www.uscis.gov/humanitarian/victims-of-human-trafficking-and-other-crimes/victims-of-human-trafficking-t-nonimmigrant-status ).
10. **Small-scale trading, importing and street or market selling (food, fabrics, beauty and electronics).** The earlier file's Path 10 covers formal importing. For informal versions I found no US data on scale or Nigerian share (GAP). Risks: customs and FDA rules on imported food, sales-tax and business-license violations, counterfeit goods, supplier fraud, cash theft.
11. **Restaurant, catering and community-business jobs (kitchens, event catering, hair/beauty supply, shops owned by co-ethnic employers).** Plausible and common in diaspora economies generally (UNVERIFIED for Nigerians). Risks: tip theft, "training wage" abuse, dependency on a single employer who knows the worker's status.
12. **Remote or online freelancing paid from abroad while in the US.** Work authorization rules apply to the person performing the work in the US, not to where the payer is (LIKELY standard position, F-1 and B visitors cannot do this lawfully). Data on Nigerians: none (GAP). Risks: payment platform account closures, tax non-filing, status violation counted against later benefit applications.

**Where data are thin (say plainly):** I found no dataset that measures informal work for Nigerian-born people specifically. The evidence is general (EPI, DOL, ITEP, CMS), sector-level (BLS wages), documented cases (DOJ trafficking prosecutions, platform fraud cases), and the Nigerian occupation mix from ACS. Unauthorized workers are not separately identifiable in ACS. Treat the sectors above as plausible exposures, not measured shares for Nigerian immigrants.

### 2.4 Labor-trafficking and exploitation cases involving Nigerian workers

- Domestic-servitude prosecutions listed in option 9 (Texas, New Jersey, Maryland, New York). The Texas Nnaji case involved nine years of compelled labor of a Nigerian widow brought to the US (https://justice.gov/opa/pr/arlington-texas-couple-convicted-forced-labor-and-other-crimes-holding-nigerian-woman ). Defendants and victims in these cases are individual people; do not generalize.
- Nurse-recruitment contract litigation (indentured-servitude-style repayment clauses) involves Filipino nurses (Villarin, Albany Medical Center, TGS) and not, as far as I found, Nigerian nurses (https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/legal-regulatory-issues/international-nurses-sue-staffing-agency-over-quitting-penalties-wages , https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/firms-to-pay-10m-in-case-claiming-essentially-indentured-servitude ). Use as an analogous risk for Nigerian EB-3 nurses: penalty-for-quitting clauses (GAP: no Nigeria case found).
- DOL Wage and Hour data on Nigerian workers: NOT FOUND.

### 2.5 Enforcement actions that affect this group (what the data show)

- Removals of Nigerians have been in the low hundreds a year (138 in FY2024), with thousands on the non-detained docket (3,690 in Jan 2025), per news citing ICE (LIKELY).
- Employer sanctions and E-Verify: see 2.2.
- June 2025 interim rule speeding employer penalties: see 2.2.
- Overstay detection is data-driven (entry-exit matching), and overstay histories are cited in visa and ban decisions (Proclamation 10998 cites Nigerian rates).
- Visa bonds and the 2025 reciprocity and 2026 partial-ban measures target future entries, not people already in the US.

### 2.6 Tax and banking limits

See 2.2. Summary: ITIN filing exists for income reporting without an SSN; employers are required to verify work authorization; platform and bank onboarding generally check identity; credit building is limited; remittances to Nigeria move through licensed transfer operators (IMTO) or banks, which require ID (UNVERIFIED for status-limited Nigerians). Remittance volume to Nigeria is large (earlier file).

### 2.7 How people exit informal status (outcomes the data support)

1. **Adjustment of status through a US-citizen spouse, parent or child.** OHSS: 8,670 Nigerian-born spouses of citizens, 3,760 parents and 2,950 children of citizens became LPRs in FY2024 (up from 4,720, 2,200 and 1,360 in FY2015). Immediate relatives who entered with inspection generally may adjust despite overstay (INA 245 exception; LIKELY rule). Risks: marriage-fraud screening, interview credibility, USCIS holds for travel-ban nationals (earlier file; physicians exempted), public-charge review.
2. **Employment-based adjustment** (only for people who maintained status or qualify under 245(k)); generally not available to long-term overstayers (LIKELY rule). EB-2 and EB-3 volumes in 1.2.
3. **Asylum or withholding** for Nigerians with fear-based claims (religion, gender-based violence, political or ethnic persecution, northern insurgency exposure). News counts: about 897 Nigerians granted asylum over two years and about 1,372 over three (EOIR-based press; UNVERIFIED rates): https://thenigerialawyer.com/over-1370-nigerians-granted-us-asylum-in-three-years-as-approval-rate-rises/ ; one-year filing deadline applies (LIKELY rule). No reliable Nigeria asylum grant rate found (GAP).
4. **T and U visas** for trafficking and certain-crime victims (LIKELY rules at the USCIS links above); U visa annual cap 10,000 (LIKELY rule).
5. **Cancellation of removal** for long-term residents with qualifying US-citizen or LPR family (10 years presence, 4,000 a year cap, LIKELY rule) is a defensive remedy in court only.
6. **Departure and consular processing.** Leaving after more than 180 days of unlawful presence triggers a 3-year bar; more than 1 year, a 10-year bar (INA 212(a)(9)(B), LIKELY rule); waivers need a qualifying relative. Under Proclamation 10998 and the 75-country immigrant-visa pause, consular exit routes for Nigerians outside the US are largely closed in 2026, which makes in-US adjustment far more valuable (CONFIRMED scope above).
7. **Return.** Return-migration rates are NOT FOUND. Returnees face FX and cost-of-living issues (earlier file).

## 4. TWENTY MORE CHALLENGE IDEAS (route-specific)

Format: Challenge | Based on | Pass | Fail. These do not duplicate the 20 in the earlier file.

1. **Bond-airport routing.** Based on the bond pilot's designated airports (BOS, JFK, IAD, EWR, ATL, ORD, LAX; https://www.bu.edu/isso/2026/01/05/b-1-visa-bond-pilot-program-expanded/ ). Pass: the visiting parent routes through a listed airport and is admitted. Fail: books a ticket to Houston or DFW, is not admitted on the bond visa, and loses the trip.
2. **Bond forfeiture.** Based on permanent $10,000 to $20,000 tiers and refund-on-timely-departure rule (CONFIRMED rule). Pass: relative leaves before the I-94 date and the bond is cancelled. Fail: a medical delay causes a late departure and the family loses the bond.
3. **Three-month window.** Based on single-entry, 3-month validity from 2025-07-08 (CONFIRMED). Pass: plans the whole family event inside 90 days. Fail: wedding and funeral are 5 months apart and the second trip needs a new visa and bond.
4. **B-to-F shortcut.** Based on the "preconceived intent" rule and an F-1 refusal estimate near 41% (UNVERIFIED). Pass: applies for F-1 abroad with full funding. Fail: enters on B with a hidden plan, the change is denied, and the character must leave or overstays.
5. **F-1 interview funds.** Based on F-1 issuances to Nigerian nationals falling from 7,556 (FY22) to 3,939 (FY25) (LIKELY). Pass: shows a funded I-20 and a clear return plan. Fail: refusal; the semester is lost.
6. **90-day OPT clock.** Based on CONFIRMED 90 days (150 with STEM OPT). Pass: finds an employer within the window. Fail: unemployment days exhausted; status ends.
7. **STEM OPT employer check.** Based on E-Verify and training-plan requirements (CONFIRMED). Pass: confirms the employer is enrolled before accepting. Fail: employer not in E-Verify and the extension is refused.
8. **Pre-2026 visa decision.** Based on Proclamation 10998 (valid pre-2026 visas stay valid; first-time and some renewals barred, CONFIRMED/UNVERIFIED). Pass: a student already in the US stays in status and avoids leaving without a plan. Fail: leaves on a family trip, the visa cannot be renewed, and cannot return.
9. **O-1 portfolio.** Based on the USCIS three-criteria evidence test and growth from 35 to 154 O-1s (CONFIRMED rule, LIKELY data). Pass: builds press, awards, judging and a high-pay contract; an agent or employer petitions. Fail: thin evidence; denial after months of waiting.
10. **Peer advisory opinion.** Based on USCIS requirement for a peer-group consultation (CONFIRMED). Pass: gets the union or expert letter in time. Fail: festival date passes before the petition clears.
11. **EB-2 NIW self-petition.** Based on EB-2 LPRs for Nigerians rising from 410 (2015) to 1,510 (2024). Pass: well-documented research or public-interest impact; approved. Fail: "national importance" not shown; denial.
12. **EB-3 nurse spike and collapse.** Based on EB-3 principals 1,670 (2022) to 530 (2024). Pass: files early in a favorable year and uses a fee-free employer. Fail: retrogression stalls the case and the employer cancels.
13. **Quit-penalty clause.** Based on indentured-servitude-style nurse-contract litigation (Filipino nurse cases as analog; LIKELY). Pass: reads and negotiates the clause or declines the offer. Fail: signs a 36-month repayment term and is trapped in a poor job.
14. **K-1 90-day clock.** Based on 985 K-1 issuances in FY2024 and the 90-day marriage rule (LIKELY). Pass: marries on time and files adjustment. Fail: wedding slips past 90 days and the status ends.
15. **Marriage-based adjustment after overstay.** Based on 8,670 spouse LPRs in FY2024 and the immediate-relative exception (CONFIRMED data, LIKELY rule). Pass: a genuine marriage, complete evidence, successful interview. Fail: weak evidence and a fraud inquiry; removal proceedings.
16. **Unlawful-presence bars.** Based on 3-year and 10-year bars at 180 days and 1 year (LIKELY rule) and B overstay rates of 5.56% to 21.33%. Pass: fixes status before 180 days. Fail: leaves too late and cannot return for years, while the 2026 immigrant-visa pause adds delay.
17. **Live-in aide wage gap.** Based on BLS $16.78 an hour median for aides (May 2024), the 2025 DOL enforcement pullback, and state overtime rules in NY and NJ. Pass: keeps a written schedule, tracks hours, learns the state rule and moves to an agency job. Fail: works 24-hour shifts for a flat cash fee and loses wages.
18. **Account-sharing trap.** Based on DOJ's fraudulent-driver-account prosecutions and platform crackdowns (CONFIRMED). Pass: the character recognizes identity-fraud risk, declines, and uses authorized gig or employer work. Fail: borrows another person's profile, is deactivated or charged, and loses the vehicle income.
19. **Braiding license.** Based on all-state licensing (BLS) and up to 2,100 training hours in some states (IJ, advocacy). Pass: enrolls in a licensing or apprenticeship path in a state with lighter requirements, or works for a licensed owner with fair pay. Fail: fined for unlicensed practice and loses the shop booth.
20. **Recognizing trafficking and getting help.** Based on the Nnaji, Bolarinwa, Katy and Udeozor domestic-servitude prosecutions and T-visa protections (CONFIRMED cases, LIKELY rule). Pass: contacts legal aid or the national trafficking hotline and obtains protection. Fail: remains isolated because the employer controls the passport and threatens deportation.

## 5. KNOWN GAPS AND UNCERTAINTIES

1. **No Nigeria-specific informal-work dataset.** All "informal sector" statements combine BLS wages, EPI/DOL wage-theft research, ITEP tax estimates, documented DOJ cases and ACS occupation mix. I found no share of Nigerian-born workers that is unauthorized or paid in cash in any sector. Unauthorized population of Nigerian origin: NOT FOUND.
2. **Visa issuance counts are third-party.** Class-by-class numbers come from visagrader.com (republishing State Department data). FY2015 to FY2017 are missing; FY2026 is partial; refusal rates are "projected," not official. Intelpoint and academicjobs.com give different Nigerian NIV totals (87,300 vs 90,344 for 2024). Verify at the Report of the Visa Office and monthly NIV tables on travel.state.gov (403 to my tool).
3. **Overstay tables FY2015 to FY2017 not retrieved.** FY2018 to FY2024 are parsed from DHS PDFs. FY2025 report (departures through Sept 2025) not yet found; none was published in the sources I could see. All rates are upper bounds.
4. **Proclamation 10998 detail.** Family-based exemptions, renewals, and any 2026 amendment or removal of Nigeria are unconfirmed. The earlier file's note about an August 2026 court voiding of the 75-country pause is still unverified. I did not see the proclamation text or the Federal Register.
5. **Visa bond.** Permanent rule amounts ($10,000 / $15,000 / $20,000) are consistent across sources, but I did not see the final country list, the designated-airport list for the permanent rule, or whether the August 2026 pilot airport list changed.
6. **Reciprocity schedule.** The official Nigeria reciprocity page was not opened; pre-July 2025 validity (multiple-entry, multi-year) is background knowledge. Consular wait times in Lagos and Abuja remain unsourced.
7. **Diversity visa.** OHSS data show Nigerian DV green cards effectively ended after FY2015, which conflicts with the earlier file's statement that Nigerians won DV in large numbers in the 2010s and with the DV-2022 exclusion narrative. I could not find the official eligibility list for each DV year. Treat DV as closed for all arrival years.
8. **E treaty status.** Nigeria's absence from the E-1/E-2 list is supported by a news list and by the absence of Nigerian-national E-1 issuances, not by the State Department table itself.
9. **Open Doors.** Several years (2017/18 to 2019/20) not found; one source's 2021/22 figure (14,438) looks mixed up with a naturalization number.
10. **Metro data.** No official ACS metro counts for Nigerian-born in Houston, DFW, Chicago, Atlanta, New York, LA or the Bay Area; cities mix birthplace and ancestry. Only DC has a documented IIR fact sheet.
11. **Asylum.** Only press-reported counts (897 over two years, 1,372 over three); no grant rate.
12. **Rules I did not re-open on official pages:** B-to-F change of status, K-1 90 days, 212(e) and Conrad 30, unlawful-presence bars, immediate-relative adjustment exception, cancellation of removal caps, U/T visas, ITIN rules, R-1 5-year limit. They are standard law (LIKELY) and should be confirmed at uscis.gov and travel.state.gov before shipping.
13. **E-Verify state list.** Sources conflict (4, 9 or 11 states); confirm each state before hard-coding.
14. **Sensitive-topic caution.** Trafficking prosecutions and overstay figures must not be presented as typical of Nigerians; the game should show them as risks any migrant can face and give characters protective choices.
